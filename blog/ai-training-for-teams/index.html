AI TRAINING

Practical AI training for teams: start with workflows, not hype

Good AI training connects tools to the real work people need to do—while keeping human judgement, privacy and accuracy in the process.

Quick takeaway: start with the customer's problem and the outcome you want. Tools and channels should support that goal, not distract from it.

Start with repetitive work

Ask teams where they spend time drafting, summarising, researching, organising information or preparing routine materials. Choose a small number of low-risk workflows to practise.

Teach a repeatable prompting pattern

Explain the context, task, audience, constraints and preferred output. Ask the tool to state assumptions or identify missing information instead of treating every answer as correct.

Set safe-use rules

Teams should know what information must not be entered into unapproved tools, when outputs need fact-checking and which decisions require a qualified human.

Practise on realistic examples

Use anonymised scenarios from marketing, customer service, HR, operations or sales. Participants should leave with a workflow they can actually use, not only a list of tools.

Measure usefulness

Compare time, quality, consistency and rework before and after a pilot. Keep workflows that create measurable value and revise or stop those that do not.
